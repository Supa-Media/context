# Marquee quotes

We asked three marquee companies for quotes for the garden, for 110 guests seated. This page keeps the numbers side by side, with the terms that matter, so the decision is made on the same basis. The venue's own garden is the site for all three quotes, and each quote assumes the ground is dry on the day.

## Company one: Northfield Marquees

Quote for a 12 by 24 metre marquee with a wooden floor, lighting on the poles and tables and chairs for 110.

- Hire: 2,650 pounds, including delivery and the crew on the day.
- Floor: the wooden floor is an extra 600 pounds, and it is needed on any ground that is not level.
- Lighting: festoon lights on the poles, 240 pounds.
- Power: a generator is extra, 180 pounds, if the venue's supply is not free for the garden.

Northfield are the most flexible about dates and will move the booking once for free if the wedding changes. Their crew comes two hours before the guests and leaves an hour after the last song, which suits the timeline.

## Company two: Hollin and Pryce

Quote for the same size, with a clear-span frame and no poles, which gives a better view of the garden from the seats.

- Hire: 2,990 pounds, including delivery and the crew.
- Floor: included.
- Lighting: included, but only the bulbs, not the festoon.
- Power: included, with a generator on standby.

The clear-span frame is the nicest option for the photographs. Hollin and Pryce are the most expensive, but the floor and the power are in the price, which makes the total close to Northfield's once Northfield's extras are added.

## Company three: Green Lane Events

Quote for a smaller marquee, 10 by 20 metres, which is tight for 110 seated but fine for 96.

- Hire: 2,100 pounds, delivery extra.
- Floor: 500 pounds.
- Lighting: 200 pounds.
- Power: the venue's supply, free.

Green Lane are the cheapest, but the marquee is small. They offered a larger one for an extra 800 pounds, which brings them above Northfield.

## The deposit terms

This is the part that decides it. Each company asks for a deposit at booking, and the rules on refunds are very different.

The marquee company's deposit is non-refundable after 1 December. Northfield's deposit of 30 per cent is refundable in full until 1 December if the wedding changes, and it is not refundable after that date. The balance is due fourteen days before the wedding. Hollin and Pryce take a 40 per cent deposit that is refundable only until the end of September. Green Lane take 25 per cent, refundable until 1 November, with a 50 pound admin fee.

The group chat asked whether any company would let the deposit roll into next year. Only Northfield said yes, and only once.

## Weather

All three quotes include a wet-weather plan. Northfield's is a second tent on standby at 400 pounds, to be ordered 48 hours before the day. Hollin and Pryce include it free. Green Lane do not offer one, which is a reason to ask about a backup at the venue's own room.

## Recommendation

The honest answer is that Northfield is the best value if the date is still open and the floor is not needed, and Hollin and Pryce is the best if the photographs matter most. Green Lane is ruled out by the size. The decision goes to the planning call on 12 November, and the deposit has to be paid before 1 December in every case, so the answer is needed before then.

## Open questions

- Ask the venue whether the garden floor is level enough to skip the wooden floor.
- Ask whether the wedding can use the venue's own power for the garden lights.
- Check the insurance requirements for a marquee on the lawn; the venue has a form.
- Confirm the number of guests in the garden after the dinner, because the seated count is not the whole crowd.

## Site visit notes

The site visit was on a dry Tuesday, which is not the day of the wedding. The garden drains well at the top end and badly by the wall, where the water sits after rain. Whichever company is chosen will need to put the marquee on the top half, and the bar and the buffet in the lower half, which the venue's own plan already shows.

The power outlet by the potting shed is on a separate breaker from the house. Two of the three companies asked whether it could take a generator's cable, and the venue said yes in principle but wants the electrician to check it the week before. That check is a cost nobody has put on any of the quotes yet.

The gate to the garden is wide enough for the flatbed on Northfield's quote, but not for the larger lorry that Hollin and Pryce would bring. They said they would bring the smaller frame on a second trip, which adds a delivery charge that is not in the quote above.

## Guests and tables

The count of 110 seated is the head count after the last list. The garden will also take the evening guests who come after the dinner, which is a crowd of about forty people. The companies were asked to quote for the seated count only, so the evening crowd is an open cost. The venue's bar service can handle it, but the bar is in the lower half, and the crowd will need to walk past the buffet.

Table sizes differ between the companies. Northfield quoted round tables of ten, which fits the seating plan. Hollin and Pryce quoted long tables, which change the seating plan completely, and Green Lane quoted round tables of eight, which means more tables and a bigger floor.

## Timeline on the day

Northfield's crew starts two hours before the guests, with the floor and the lights. The power is on at least an hour before the first guest arrives. The flat-pack tables go in during the first hour, and the chairs follow. The crew leaves an hour after the last song, which is the end time the venue has written into its contract.

Hollin and Pryce need three hours for the clear-span frame, so the start has to move earlier. The venue said this is possible but will cost the morning cleaner an extra hour, which the couple has to pay for.

## What the group asked

The group asked three questions after the quotes came in. Is the wooden floor needed? Can the garden lights run from the venue's power? What happens if it rains on the day? The answers are above and the last one is the one that should decide it: Northfield's second tent is the cheapest insurance, and it is booked forty-eight hours before the day, not at the deposit.
