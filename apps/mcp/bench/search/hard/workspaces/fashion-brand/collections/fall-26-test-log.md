# Fall 26 test log

The fabric test log for the fall collection. Every fabric that goes into a garment gets the same tests before the order is placed: shrinkage, colour fastness, pilling and the hand check the team does by feel. The results are written here so the next season can start from them. The test lab's report is attached to each supplier's folder, and this page is the summary the team reads.

## How the tests are run

Each swatch is cut to a 30 centimetre square and marked on the back with the supplier code. Shrinkage is measured in both directions before the first wash, then after one wash at the care label's temperature, then after a second wash. Colour fastness is checked against a white cloth under a daylight lamp. Pilling is rated on the standard scale after 2,000 rubs. The hand check is done by three people who do not see the supplier code.

The lab takes about ten working days. The team has agreed that no fabric is ordered until its shrinkage is known, because the pattern is cut to the finished size and a fabric that moves by four per cent ruins a jacket.

## Wool

The brushed wool from the quarry mill was tested in three weights. The lightest pilled worst, which was expected, and the heaviest held its shape best. The mill's own figures were close to ours on the first wash and further apart on the second.

- Light weight: pilling at 3 after 2,000 rubs.
- Medium weight: pilling at 4 after 2,000 rubs, the one we want for the jackets.
- Heavy weight: pilling at 4, but stiff in the hand, so not for the coats.

## Linen

The linen was the most predictable. It shrinks a little on the first wash and then settles, and the colour held on every shade except the mid-blue, which lost some depth. The hand check was positive: the team liked the linen better than the wool for the summer pieces.

## Cotton twill

The twill was tested in two finishes. The sanded finish shrank less and pilled more, and the unsanded one shrank more and held its surface. The team has not decided which to use for the workwear line, and the decision waits for the cut-and-sew partners' view on how each finish sews.

## The shrinkage results

This is the table the cutters use. The figures are the change in the longer measurement, in per cent, from the unwashed swatch.

- Brushed wool, medium weight: 2 per cent after one wash at 30 degrees, 3 per cent after the second.
- Brushed wool, heavy weight: 4 per cent after one wash at 30 degrees, 5 per cent after the second.
- Linen: 1 per cent after the first wash, 1 per cent after the second.
- Cotton twill, sanded: 2 per cent after one wash, then stable.
- Cotton twill, unsanded: 3 per cent after one wash, then stable.

The heavy-weight brushed wool shrinks 4 percent after one wash at 30 degrees, and the medium weight shrinks 2 percent under the same wash. The cutters use the medium weight figure for the jackets and add the allowance on the pattern, not on the fabric order.

## Colour fastness

Every dye lot passed the standard for daylight. The darkest navy failed the wet rub test on one lot, which was reported to the supplier and replaced. The rest of the palette is clean, and the team asked for the same lab check on every new lot, which adds about a day to the order.

## Notes for the next season

Ask the mills for their shrinkage figures before the sample stage, not after. Put the care-label wash into the test schedule from the start. Keep the swatches for two seasons, because a customer return is the only time anyone looks at them again.

The cut-and-sew partners asked for the test results in the same format every season. The format on this page is the one to copy.

## Sample sizes and repeats

Each fabric is tested on three swatches cut from the same roll, and the average is the number on this page. The lab asked for five swatches on the wool, because the brushed finish varies more across the roll than the linen does. The team kept three for the cost and checked the wool's spread by eye. A later season should use five.

Each test is repeated after a week to make sure the first result was not a fluke. The wool's second reading was within half a per cent of the first, which is good enough for the jackets. The twill's second reading was further apart, and the team has written that down as the reason the sanded finish is still open.

## Care labels

The care labels in the collection were written before these tests. Some of them say a wash temperature that the fabric does not need, and one of them, on the brushed wool coat, says to tumble dry, which the test showed shrinks the coat by more than the label admits. The labels are being rewritten for the spring run. The new wording is in the product folder.

The team also asked whether the cold wash should be on the label. The answer is yes for the linen and no for the wool, because the cold cycle does not get the dust out of the brushed finish. The labels will say so in plain words.

## Lab notes

The lab's own notes are attached to the supplier folder. They include the photographs of the pilled swatches, the water-bath results and the daylight lamp readings. The lab also warned that the navy dye lot had a faint halo after the rub test, which was not a fail on its own but was enough for the team to ask for a new lot.

The lab's turnaround is ten working days, and the team booked a second slot for the spring samples in case the first one slips. The second slot is free and nobody has used it yet.

## Decisions so far

- Wool: medium weight for the jackets, heavy weight for nothing this season.
- Linen: approved for the summer pieces at the current supplier.
- Twill: sanded finish is provisional until the cut-and-sew partners report on sewing.
- Navy: new lot needed; the supplier has agreed to replace it.
- Care labels: rewritten for the spring run, with the cold wash named for the linen only.

## Pattern allowances

The pattern room uses the shrinkage table to set the allowance on each block. A jacket block in the medium brushed wool gets two per cent added to the length and the width of the body, and the sleeves get the same figure on the length only. The heavy-weight wool does not go into the jackets, so its four per cent never reaches a block, but it is kept on the table because the coats were drafted for it before the change.

The linen blocks are cut at one per cent, which is almost nothing, but the team keeps the allowance because the summer pieces are sewn by a partner who presses hard. The twill is cut at two per cent for the sanded finish and three for the unsanded, which is the one number on the table that changes with the choice still open.

## Who does what

The lab is booked by the production manager. The swatches are cut by the sample room. The tests are read by the head of product and the cutter, and the hand check is run by whoever is in the studio that week, with the supplier codes covered. The results are written up in this log by the production manager, and the head of product signs them off before any order goes to a mill.
